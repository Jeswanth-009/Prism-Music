    // 08 — faq

    Questions,
    answered

    01Why isn't this on the Play Store?

    Prism Music streams from public sources and is distributed as an open-source APK. Sideloading takes one extra tap during install — and it's the reason the app can stay free, ad-free and account-free.

    02Is it really free? What's the catch?

    There's no catch. The app is MIT-licensed open source and the builds are generated automatically from the public repository. No ads, no subscriptions, no data collection.

    03Where does the music come from?

    Prism searches public YouTube Music and JioSaavn catalogs and streams the audio directly, with multiple fallback sources for reliability. Nothing is hosted by Prism itself.

    04Is my iPhone / Windows PC supported?

    Prism is Android-first today. The Flutter codebase can target iOS and desktop later, but Android is the platform that's tested and shipped.

    05Can I use it offline?

    Yes — download songs inside the app and they play without a connection. Everything else (search, streaming, recommendations) needs internet.

    06Will updates break my library?

    No. Your library is stored on your device and updates install over the top. Prism also writes a backup to your device storage as you listen, and a fresh install restores from it automatically.

    07How do I import a playlist?

    Copy a Spotify or YouTube playlist link and paste it into Library → Import. Prism matches the tracks it can find and saves the playlist locally — no account linking required.

    08Where do my backups live?

    In your device's shared storage, under PrismMusic/backup/. The file is plain JSON on purpose — you can read it, copy it, or move it to a new phone yourself.

    09Why can quality or availability vary?

    Streams come from public third-party endpoints (YouTube, JioSaavn, Piped/Invidious mirrors). When one source is slow or down, Prism falls through to the next — which can change bitrate or availability between builds.
